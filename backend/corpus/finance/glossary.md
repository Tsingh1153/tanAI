# Finance Glossary

Plain-English definitions of common finance terms, spanning personal finance,
markets, and corporate finance. Educational reference only.

- **APR (Annual Percentage Rate)**: the yearly cost of borrowing, including
  interest and certain fees, expressed as a percentage.
- **APY (Annual Percentage Yield)**: the yearly return on savings including
  compounding; higher than the nominal rate when interest compounds.
- **Asset**: something of value you own (cash, investments, property).
- **Liability**: something you owe (loans, credit card balances).
- **Equity**: assets minus liabilities — your net ownership stake.
- **Liquidity**: how quickly an asset can be converted to cash without losing value.
- **Diversification**: spreading investments to reduce exposure to any single risk.
- **Compounding**: earning returns on prior returns, not just the original amount.
- **Inflation**: the general rise in prices over time, which erodes purchasing power.
- **Dividend**: a share of company profits paid to stockholders.
- **Capital gain**: profit from selling an asset for more than you paid.
- **Bull / bear market**: sustained rising / falling market conditions.
- **Volatility**: the degree to which a price fluctuates.
- **Bond yield**: the return earned on a bond, related inversely to its price.
- **ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)**: a fund holding many securities that trades like
  a stock.
- **Index fund**: a fund that tracks a market index rather than picking holdings.
- **Expense ratio**: a fund's annual fee as a percentage of assets.
- **Net income**: profit after all expenses, interest, and taxes.
- **Revenue**: total sales before any expenses ("top line").
- **EBIT / EBITDA**: earnings before interest and taxes / before interest, taxes,
  depreciation, and amortization.
- **Free cash flow**: cash a company generates after capital expenditures.
- **WACC**: weighted average cost of capital, used to discount future cash flows.
- **Present value**: today's worth of a future sum, discounted at a rate.
- **Marginal tax rate**: the rate applied to your next dollar of income.
- **Effective tax rate**: total tax divided by total income.
- **Principal**: the original amount borrowed or invested, excluding interest.
- **Amortization**: paying off debt in scheduled installments, or spreading an
  intangible asset's cost over time.
