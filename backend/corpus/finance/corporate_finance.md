# Corporate Finance & Accounting Reference

Educational reference for analysts and students. General information, not advice.

## The three financial statements

- **Income statement**: revenue minus expenses over a period → net income.
  Order: Revenue − COGS = Gross profit; − Operating expenses = Operating income
  (EBIT); − Interest & taxes = Net income.
- **Balance sheet**: a snapshot at a point in time. **Assets = Liabilities +
  Shareholders' equity** (the accounting equation).
- **Cash flow statement**: cash movements in three sections — Operating,
  Investing, Financing. Reconciles net income (accrual) to actual cash.

How they link: net income flows into retained earnings (equity) on the balance
sheet and starts the cash flow statement; the ending cash balance appears as an
asset on the balance sheet.

## Accrual vs. cash accounting

Accrual accounting records revenue when earned and expenses when incurred,
regardless of cash timing; cash accounting records them when cash changes hands.
Accrual gives a truer picture of performance but can diverge from cash, which is
why the cash flow statement matters.

## Debits and credits

Double-entry bookkeeping: every transaction affects at least two accounts and
debits equal credits. Debits increase assets and expenses; credits increase
liabilities, equity, and revenue.

## Ratio analysis

- **Liquidity**: Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities; Quick ratio
  excludes inventory.
- **Leverage**: Debt-to-equity = total debt ÷ equity; Interest coverage = EBIT ÷
  interest expense.
- **Profitability**: Gross margin, Operating margin, Net margin; ROE = net income
  ÷ equity; ROA = net income ÷ assets.
- **Efficiency**: Inventory turnover, Receivables days.

Always compare ratios to the company's history and industry peers.

## Time value of money

A dollar today is worth more than a dollar later because it can earn a return.

- **Present value**: PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)^n
- **Future value**: FV = PV × (1 + r)^n

where r is the periodic rate and n the number of periods.

## Valuation

- **Discounted cash flow (DCF)**: project future free cash flows, discount them to
  present value at the WACC, and sum (plus a terminal value) to estimate intrinsic
  value.
- **WACC** (weighted average cost of capital): blends the after-tax cost of debt
  and cost of equity, weighted by their share of the capital structure.
- **Comparables (multiples)**: value a company relative to peers using ratios such
  as EV/EBITDA or P/E.

### Worked example — present value

Future value $1,000 in 3 years at 5% annual rate:
PV = 1000 ÷ (1.05)^3 = 1000 ÷ 1.157625 ≈ **$863.84**.
