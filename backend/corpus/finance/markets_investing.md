# Markets & Investing Reference

Educational reference on public markets and investing concepts. General
information only; not investment advice, and not a recommendation to buy or sell
any security. Past performance does not predict future results.

## Asset classes

- **Stocks (equities)**: ownership shares in a company; returns from price changes
  and dividends. Higher expected return, higher volatility.
- **Bonds (fixed income)**: loans to a government or company that pay interest;
  generally lower risk than stocks. Prices fall when interest rates rise.
- **Cash & equivalents**: money-market funds, T-bills; low risk, low return.
- **Funds**: pooled vehicles holding many securities — mutual funds and
  exchange-traded funds (ETFs).

## Index vs. active investing

- **Index (passive)**: buy a fund tracking a broad index (e.g. an S&P 500 ETF).
  Low fees, broad diversification, matches the market minus costs.
- **Active**: a manager picks holdings aiming to beat a benchmark. Higher fees;
  most active funds underperform their index over long horizons after costs.

## Diversification and risk

Diversification spreads money across many holdings so no single failure is
catastrophic. It reduces *idiosyncratic* (company-specific) risk but not *market*
risk. Key risk ideas:

- **Volatility**: how much returns fluctuate (often measured by standard deviation).
- **Risk tolerance vs. capacity**: willingness vs. financial ability to bear loss.
- **Time horizon**: longer horizons can tolerate more volatility.

## Common valuation metrics

- **Market capitalization** = share price × shares outstanding (company's equity value).
- **P/E ratio** = price per share ÷ earnings per share; a rough gauge of how much
  investors pay per dollar of earnings.
- **Dividend yield** = annual dividend ÷ share price.
- **EPS** = net income ÷ shares outstanding.

These are context-dependent; compare within an industry, not across unrelated ones.

## How orders work

- **Market order**: execute now at the best available price.
- **Limit order**: execute only at a specified price or better.
- **Bid/ask spread**: the gap between the highest buy and lowest sell price;
  a transaction cost, wider for illiquid securities.

## Core principles many educators cite

- Costs and taxes compound against you; minimize fees.
- Time in the market generally beats timing the market.
- Dollar-cost averaging (investing fixed amounts on a schedule) removes the need
  to predict short-term moves.
- Rebalancing periodically returns a portfolio to its target mix.

## What this assistant will not do
