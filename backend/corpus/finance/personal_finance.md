# Personal Finance Reference

Educational reference for individual money management. This is general information,
not personalized financial advice.

## Budgeting

A budget assigns every dollar of income to a purpose. Two common frameworks:

- **50/30/20**: 50% of after-tax income to needs (housing, food, utilities,
  minimum debt payments), 30% to wants, 20% to saving and extra debt payoff.
- **Zero-based budgeting**: income minus allocations equals zero — every dollar is
  given a job, including saving.

Track spending for a month before setting targets; real numbers beat estimates.

## Emergency fund

A cash reserve for unexpected expenses (job loss, medical bills, car repair). A
common target is three to six months of essential expenses, held in a liquid,
low-risk account such as a high-yield savings account (HYSA). Build a small
starter buffer (e.g. one month) before aggressively paying down non-urgent debt.

## Debt payoff

Two popular strategies for multiple debts:

- **Avalanche**: pay minimums on all debts, then put extra toward the highest
  interest rate first. Mathematically minimizes total interest paid.
- **Snowball**: pay extra toward the smallest balance first for quick wins and
  motivation. Costs slightly more interest but improves follow-through.

High-interest debt (credit cards, often 20%+ APR) usually deserves priority over
extra investing, because paying it off is a guaranteed return equal to the rate.

## Credit scores

A credit score (e.g. FICO, 300–850) estimates credit risk. Major factors:

- **Payment history** (~35%): on-time payments matter most.
- **Credit utilization** (~30%): balances owed vs. limits; keeping utilization
  low (often cited as under ~30%) helps.
- **Length of credit history** (~15%).
- **Credit mix** (~10%) and **new credit / inquiries** (~10%).

## Account types

- **Checking**: everyday transactions; little or no interest.
- **Savings / HYSA**: interest-bearing, liquid; good for emergency funds.
- **Brokerage**: holds investments (stocks, ETFs, funds); taxable.
- **Certificates of deposit (CDs)**: fixed term, fixed rate.

## Retirement accounts (U.S.)

- **401(k)/403(b)**: employer-sponsored; contributions often pre-tax; many
  employers match a percentage (a match is effectively free money).
- **Traditional IRA**: contributions may be tax-deductible; growth tax-deferred;
  withdrawals taxed in retirement.
- **Roth IRA / Roth 401(k)**: contributions are after-tax; qualified withdrawals
  are tax-free. Roth is often favored when you expect a higher tax rate later.

Contribution limits and rules change yearly; verify current figures with the IRS.

## Taxes (basics)

- **Marginal vs. effective rate**: the marginal rate applies to your last dollar
  of income; the effective rate is total tax divided by total income and is lower.
- **Deductions** reduce taxable income; **credits** reduce tax owed dollar-for-dollar.
- **Tax-advantaged accounts** (401k, IRA, HSA) can lower current or future taxes.

## Insurance and protection

Insurance transfers the risk of large, rare losses. Common types: health, auto,
renters/homeowners, term life (if others depend on your income), and disability.
Match coverage to real exposures rather than over-insuring low-cost risks.
